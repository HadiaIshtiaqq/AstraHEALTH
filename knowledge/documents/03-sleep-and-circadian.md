# Sleep and Circadian Rhythm in Spaceflight

> ASTRAHEALTH educational note (team-authored summary for a hackathon prototype). Not an official NASA document.

## Sleep loss is common in flight
Crew members on orbit have often obtained less sleep than on Earth. Contributing factors include workload, schedule shifts, noise from equipment, temperature, light exposure, motion sickness early in flight, and stress.

## Circadian disruption
Without a natural 24-hour day, light exposure and schedule changes can shift the body clock. Lighting schedules, consistent sleep periods and planned work-rest cycles are used to support circadian alignment.

## Effects of insufficient sleep
Repeated short sleep can affect alertness, mood, reaction time and the ability to perform demanding tasks. Fatigue reports are more informative when they are recorded alongside sleep duration and quality.

## Practical sleep countermeasures
Protecting scheduled sleep periods, controlling light before sleep, reducing noise, keeping the sleep station comfortable and reviewing persistent short-sleep patterns with the medical team are common sleep countermeasures. Decisions about sleep medication belong to qualified medical staff.

## Sleep duration versus sleep quality
Sleep duration and sleep quality are different measures. A long sleep period can still be poor quality; a self-reported quality score should be recorded separately from duration.
