# Behavioral Health, Isolation and Confinement

> ASTRAHEALTH educational note (team-authored summary for a hackathon prototype). Not an official NASA document.

## Isolation and confinement
Long missions involve living and working in a small space with a small crew, far from family and friends, with limited privacy. These conditions can affect mood, stress, motivation, sleep and team dynamics over time.

## Monitoring wellbeing with self-reports
Regular, brief self-reports of mood, stress and fatigue can reveal gradual changes that are easy to miss day to day. Self-report scales used in a prototype are not validated psychological instruments, and a change in scores is not a diagnosis.

## Support and countermeasures
Supportive measures include meaningful work, maintaining routines, exercise, sleep protection, scheduled private communication with family, recreational time, and access to behavioral health professionals. A private, non-judgmental conversation with the crew medical officer is an appropriate step when several wellbeing measures such as mood, stress and fatigue change together.

## Privacy of wellbeing data
Behavioral health information is sensitive. Crew members should control who sees self-reported wellbeing data, and systems should not log free-text entries unnecessarily.
