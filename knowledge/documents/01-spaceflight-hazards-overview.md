# Spaceflight Hazards Overview

> ASTRAHEALTH educational note (team-authored summary for a hackathon prototype). Not an official NASA document. Consult the referenced public NASA sources for authoritative content.

## Five hazard categories
NASA's Human Research Program groups the hazards of human spaceflight into five categories, often remembered as RIDGE: space Radiation, Isolation and confinement, Distance from Earth, Gravity fields (altered gravity), and hostile or closed Environments. These hazards act together over long missions, which is why monitoring looks at several body systems at once rather than one measurement in isolation.

## Why combined monitoring matters
Changes in sleep, mood, exercise routine and cardiovascular measurements can occur during the same period. Seeing them together helps a crew member and the medical team decide which measurements to repeat and which trends need human review. Co-occurring changes do not by themselves establish a cause.

## Distance from Earth
On long-duration missions, communication with ground medical support may be delayed, and evacuation may not be possible. Crew members therefore rely on onboard medical capability, structured self-assessment and clear escalation procedures. Time-sensitive concerns should be escalated to the onboard medical officer first.

## Closed environment
The habitat atmosphere, noise, lighting and confined living space are controlled but not natural. Environmental conditions can affect sleep and wellbeing and should be recorded as context when interpreting trends.
